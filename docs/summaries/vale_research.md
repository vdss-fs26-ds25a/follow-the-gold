# Key Takeaways

## Central Banks After the Inflation Wave

- Most aggressive rate-hiking cycle in 40 years → central banks analyzing mistakes
- **More flexible 2% target**: more time to reach target to limit economic damage
- Minority (Blanchard, Constancio) calls for higher targets — controversial
- Inflation will stay structurally higher than pre-pandemic (Carney)
- **QE more sparingly**: only 40% of economists expect QE at previous scale
- QE side effects: market distortions (SVB), >£200bn in losses at BoE
- Unwinding balance sheets is uncharted territory, high risk of errors
- **Monetary–fiscal coordination** remains problematic
- Governments avoid spending cuts out of fear of losing elections
- Powell: Fed does not rely on fiscal cooperation
- BIS calls for more focus on structural policy

## Gold as an Inflation Hedge

- Gold = traditional safe haven, but **not risk-free**
- Key factor: relationship to real US Treasury yields
  - Real yields ↑ → Gold ↓
  - Real yields ↓ → Gold ↑
- **Currency dependency** is central:
  - vs. USD: losses
  - vs. JPY: +14%
  - vs. GBP: +8.6%
  - vs. EUR: +4.3%
- Gold increasingly behaves like a **risk asset**, not a classical safe haven
- Historically strong in recessions (doubled 2007–2011, Covid highs)
- Current environment (aggressive rate hikes) is atypical
- Gold shines especially during **supply-side shocks**, when inflation persists despite rate hikes
